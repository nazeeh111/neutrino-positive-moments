## Verdict

The source defensibly documents a narrow theoretical result. I found no consequential error in the sector reduction, azimuthal factor, transform normalization, Hausdorff-to-disk construction, finite candidate criterion, sharp imaginary-axis envelopes, explicit attaining measures, or spherical smoothing argument. This is an independent source-level audit, not a formal proof or an independent reproduction of Arb integrations.

The larger goal, a genuinely novel and useful scientific algorithm, is **not yet established**. The exact specialized frequency-feasibility result and sharp envelopes are plausible contributions; the saved evidence does not establish their priority or practical advantage. The draft mostly says this honestly. A successful PDF build, 504 exact classifications and independent model-agent review do not close those gaps.

## Consequential findings

1. **The closest direct mathematical predecessor should be cited and mapped, rather than left only in a task-local audit.** At draft lines 51 and 117, di Dio is cited as a general predecessor. Its Proposition 5.13 already describes the exact truncated finite-interval Stieltjes range as a region bounded by circular arcs. More directly, Heide, Fritzsche, Kirstein and Mädler (2024), *Weyl Sets in a Non-degenerate Truncated Matricial Hausdorff Moment Problem*, explicitly represents the attainable transform values as the intersection of two matrix balls with parameters constructed from the moment data. The scalar two-disk construction is therefore a specialization of existing transform-set theory. Translating one species' set and intersecting it with the other is an immediate composition once the scalar dispersion reduction is available. The draft disclaims new generic moment theory, so this does not invalidate its claim; nevertheless the new application should be separated explicitly from this known construction. The strongest priority candidates are the specialized sharp envelopes and constructive matched-second-moment instability, not the generic existence of a disk algorithm.

   Primary sources: [di Dio, Sec. 5 and Proposition 5.13](https://arxiv.org/html/1506.06589v1#S5); [Heide et al., abstract and introduction](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11785-024-01525-1).

2. **Usefulness remains an unpassed scientific requirement, not a manuscript-polish issue.** The screen contains synthetic controls and one normalized published fiducial input. Its counts agree with the report: 72 possible, 432 impossible, zero single-species false positives. The chosen generic numerical proposer also resolves all 504, and all sampled grids agree. The boundary stress correctly demonstrates exact continuous feasibility where a particular fixed grid cannot represent the attaining support. That proves a capability advantage over those grids at constructed extrema. It does not establish an advantage over classical Weyl-set evaluation or an optimized continuous convex method, nor that relevant transport data yield useful exclusions beyond known bounds. The draft correctly avoids claiming those advantages. Completing the user's original scientific goal requires either a concrete meaningful use case with documented inputs and a strong continuous comparison, or a defensible theoretical significance argument showing why the specialized envelopes answer an unresolved physics question. More grid points or presentation work would not resolve this.

3. **The manuscript is not yet a self-contained reproducibility handoff.** Section 6 gives counts and timings but no executable command, environment version, artifact manifest or mapping from claims to saved proofs. Local code and source hashes exist, so this is readily repairable without publication. A short local supplement/README should identify `verify_all.py`, screen/stress/smooth JSONs, exact dependencies and versions, and distinguish the standard-library proof replay from the Arb integration check. The smooth SciPy audit is currently described in prose; its independent implementation is not retained as a runnable script in the reviewed file inventory. Saving that implementation would strengthen reproducibility. This is a delivery gap, not evidence that the reported roots are false.

## Checks that survived

- Expanding the recurrence gives exactly `k²D_L=A_L J-B_L` and `2k²T=A_T J-B_T`; `J=(S_+-S_-)/k` gives the stated target. The longitudinal exceptional denominator argument is valid because the low moments are real.
- The transform reweighting preserves positivity on the compact support. The two degree-three localizing matrices are the correct conditions, including singular and endpoint cases. Candidate membership can be decided in one real quadratic field even when later node formulas need a further radical.
- For equal masses and zero flux, the centered lens is centrally symmetric. Its horizontal half-width is `r-c`, so the difference body has half-width `2(r-c)`. This gives both displayed bounds. The reflected two-atom family supplies sufficiency for every positive sub-bound rate. None of this proves a bound for roots with nonzero real part, and the draft says so prominently.
- The spherical kernel normalization gives the stated axial density. The initial species have zero dipole and quadrupole, so rotational convolution preserves the matched full second number tensor. Positive smooth kernels remove the singular-measure concern without establishing astrophysical relevance.
- The fiducial moments are traceable to Froustey et al., Table 1: equal electron-species number densities with axial flux factors ±1/3 and zero heavy species. The source is a test case, not an actual merger snapshot. The draft makes the distinction. [Primary source](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.11968v3#S4).
- Current screen and stress source hashes all match the saved reports; the smooth source hash also matches. Saved stress evidence includes exact Farkas witnesses for each of the three grids at both unit-scale boundaries. The saved replay summary reports 504 screen and 90 stress exact checks, plus 520 numerical certificates, 226 grid atomic witnesses and 44 grid Farkas checks. I inspected these records but did not rerun the full computation.
- Number weighting, pointwise heavy-flavor cancellation, reduced wave number, existential per-frequency quantifiers, lack of conclusions for unbracketed concentrations, and lack of a controlled speedup claim are all stated adequately.

## Scope of novelty judgment

Johns and Nagakura already distinguish a closure from a full angular completion and explicitly compare different completions with the same supplied low moments. Thus completion ambiguity alone is not a discovery. Their result does not, on this inspection, supply the present exact frequency feasibility classifier or these sharp fixed-k formulas. [Primary source, Secs. IV–V](https://arxiv.org/html/2104.04106v2#S4).

I did not locate a primary-source duplicate of the two specialized envelopes in this bounded search. That is an unresolved priority question, not affirmative evidence that they are first. A model-agent review or mandatory external-human endorsement is not itself the missing scientific ingredient; what is missing is an adequately documented comparison with the closest work and a substantive use/significance result.

Recommended disposition: retain as an honest, mathematically surviving research draft; address the direct prior-art mapping and reproducibility handoff. Do not mark the broader novel-and-useful-algorithm objective complete on this evidence.

## Follow-up: retained numerical reproduction and precise prior-art mapping

At the lead agent's request, added `independent_scipy_smooth.py` and ran it once with the existing candidate's read-only environment. The successful output is `results/independent_scipy_smooth.json`, recording Python 3.12.15, NumPy 2.3.5, SciPy 1.18.1, source/input hashes, six moment profiles and four direct complex-determinant roots. All roots fall inside the saved Arb brackets. Maximum moment deviation is 2.22e-16; maximum determinant residual in this retained implementation is 3.61e-16. It imports no production module and uses SciPy's Bessel function and separate I0/I1/I2 integrations. This resolves the missing retained SciPy implementation noted in finding 3. It does not independently certify Arb's interval arithmetic.

Precise Heide et al. correspondence: Theorem 14.2 treats a strictly positive/nondegenerate matrix Hausdorff sequence s0,...,s(2n+1), n>=0, on [alpha,beta]. At each upper-half-plane point its attainable transform values equal two explicitly defined matrix balls' intersection. Taking q=1, n=0 gives the scalar nondegenerate mass/flux transform-locus problem here; their kernel (x-z)^(-1) is the negative of this manuscript's (z-u)^(-1). Their theorem does not directly supply this implementation's degenerate zero-mass/endpoint handling or the specialized neutrino envelopes.
