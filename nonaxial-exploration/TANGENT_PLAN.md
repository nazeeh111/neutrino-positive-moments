# Targeted tangent test at a sharp axial attainer

This is a new local falsifier, not an expansion of the stopped rotated-tetrahedron scan. No universal theorem or utility claim is inferred from a negative outcome.

Fix N=1, each species mean zero and second tensor I/3, wave vector k=2 along z. The known sharp axial cap is 2/3. Use the rational sphere nodes consisting of the six coordinate axes and every signed permutation of (2/3,2/3,1/3), 30 nodes total. The plus background has mass 3/16 at each (±2/3,±2/3,1/3) and mass 1/4 at (0,0,-1). The minus background is its full inversion. These are valid full isotropic moments and admit omega=2i/3.

Compute the first derivative of this simple eigenvalue with respect to the plus weights, with minus weights constrained to be their inverted copy. Optimize that linear derivative over the positive weight polytope with the same exact moments on these fixed nodes. This explores all feasible finite changes as tangent directions from the attainer, not just random perturbations. The numerical LP is a discovery tool, not a certificate.

Decisive outcomes: a positive derivative must be checked with exact rational left/right eigenvectors and a feasible rational perturbation before claiming a local counterexample; any actual growing root above 2/3 needs a separate original-dispersion check and rigorous root certificate. A nonpositive optimal derivative closes only this first-order, fixed-node, inversion-paired route. It proves neither local optimality to second order nor the full nonaxial bound. No additional grid, larger node catalogue or utility threshold will be substituted to manufacture a pass.
