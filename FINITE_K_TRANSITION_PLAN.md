# Remaining finite-wave-number transition: proof target

2026-11-01. This is a new mathematical hypothesis, not a renewed utility or speed campaign. The conic comparison, failed fixed counterflux usefulness gate, uncertainty audit and negative fusion pilot remain unchanged. No physical ultraviolet cutoff, practical astrophysical benefit or priority claim follows from imposing a finite mathematical k interval.

For two positive axial measures with each mass N>0, each flux zero, and equal second number moment Na, 0<a<1, seek the exact maximum growth over all real frequency parts and 0<k<=K. The accepted finite-k-independent.md proves a global cubic upper bound, attainment in a low-Q band and the unrestricted-cap regime. The transition outside those regimes is explicitly open.

New mechanism under test: use BOTH classical M2 disks, rather than the enclosing Gram disk alone. On the imaginary frequency axis, their common vertical centers allow exact maximum horizontal chord lengths. Put y=gamma/k, c=1 longitudinal or2 transverse. The proposed maximum U difference is

- N(1-a)/y if y<=a;
- Na(1+y²)/[y(a+y²)] if y+a/y<=1-a;
- 2Na(1-a)/(a²+y²) otherwise.

The second condition has a solution only for a<=3−2sqrt(2). This gives candidate constant, cubic and endpoint-family pieces for the fixed-band growth bound. Necessary disk conditions are not automatically sufficient; each claimed boundary needs an explicit positive measure or an existing exact independent M2 proof.

Critical falsifier: nonzero real frequency might admit a wider horizontal chord even though the enclosing Gram radius decreases away from the axis. Do not assume axis optimality from that radius. Use independent numerical attacks only to find counterexamples, not to prove a global optimization. Success requires an analytic all-real upper bound, every support/moment constraint, sharp attainments and edge/transition consistency for the entire stated (a,K) domain. A counterexample rejects the proposed full formula; an axis-only theorem must not be passed off as closing the full problem.

No large implementation, paper update, archive replacement, changed performance threshold or extra physical scan is warranted before the proof survives independent attack. Production exact M2 geometry and the independent Hausdorff checker may check specific candidates, but neither finite samples nor a generic solver provide the missing universal theorem.
